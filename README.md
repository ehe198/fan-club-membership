A real membership solution for music artists isn't just a paywall. It's the infrastructure for a small business. That includes:

- **Recurring billing** that handles failed payments and card updates automatically, so you're not chasing expired cards
- **Gated content** that lets you tier access — free teaser, $5 tier for early listens, $25 tier for stems and session files
- **Member profiles and a directory** so fans can find each other, and so collaborators (producers, engineers, session players) can list services
- **Email marketing** built in, because you can't run a membership without talking to your members regularly
- **A branded domain** with no "powered by" badge, because your fan club should look like yours, not a subdomain of a creator platform
- **Digital product sales** for one-off releases, sample packs, or course-style content that sits alongside the recurring tiers

The platforms that check all these boxes tend to fall into two camps: hosted all-in-one builders (Brilliant Directories, Memberful, Circle, Podia) and plugin stacks you bolt onto WordPress (MemberPress, Restrict Content Pro). The hosted route is faster to launch. The plugin route is cheaper to start but heavier to maintain. For a musician who already has a day job, the hosted route usually wins.

## Where the Big Names Fall Short for Musicians

It's worth being specific about why the obvious choices don't always fit.

**Patreon** is the default, and it's genuinely easy. But it takes 8–12% of pledges plus payment processing, you live on a patreon.com URL unless you pay for a custom domain workaround, and you don't get a member directory, email marketing, or digital product sales outside the pledge system. It's a tip jar with tiers, not a business platform.

**Bandcamp** is wonderful for selling music and merch, and its Bandcamp Fridays (zero platform fees on certain days) are a real gift to artists. But its subscription feature is limited, there's no gated content system, no member directory, no email marketing. It's a store, not a membership.

**Bandzoogle** is built for musicians and has membership features, but it leans toward the website-builder end. You get a clean site and store, but the membership and community tooling is lighter than what a directory-focused platform offers.

**Memberful** is a clean membership tool that integrates with WordPress and Stripe, and it's a favorite of musicians who already have a WordPress site. But it's a membership layer, not a full platform — you still need to handle the site, the email, the SEO, the hosting separately.

What's missing from most of these is the directory and community angle. A membership solution for music artists doesn't have to be just "fans pay for content." It can also be a place where producers list services, where session musicians get found, where a regional scene organizes itself. That's where a platform originally built for directories and membership communities — like Brilliant Directories — starts to look interesting.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The membership-site literature is consistent on where musicians stumble, and it's almost never the tech.

**Don't skip testing your checkout.** A music-teacher case study on TopMusic.co describes nearly launching with a broken checkout that a friend caught at the last minute. Test the full path: sign up, pay, get the gated content, receive the welcome email. Then test it on mobile.

**Don't assume you know what fans want.** If you're new to your audience, run a beta tier first. Ask them, in their language, what they'd pay for. Stems? Lessons? Early listens? Live Q&A? Don't build the $50 tier until someone has told you they'd pay $50.

**Don't launch without a content plan.** Memberships die without fresh content. Before you launch, know what you're shipping in month one, month two, month three. Have a backup plan for when you're sick or on tour. The Memberful blog puts it simply: free content draws fans in, exclusive content keeps them.

**Don't ignore churn.** The single most important number in a membership business is churn — the percentage of members who cancel each month. Track it from day one. If you're pouring new members into a bucket with a hole, you'll never grow. Brilliant Directories' Pro plan adds member analytics that help here; on lower tiers, track it manually.
